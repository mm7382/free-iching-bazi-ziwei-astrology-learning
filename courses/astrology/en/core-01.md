THE IDEA

A birth chart is a table of where things were in the sky at one moment, seen from one place.

- ASC A circle cut into twelve, with the starting point on the left. Diagram: a double ring. The outer ring is divided into twelve parts for the signs, the inner ring stands for the houses, and a horizontal line on the left is labelled ASC, the point the chart is counted from. A concept figure with no real degrees. 01A chart records the sky at the moment you were born. A chart is a table of positions Think of the chart as a seating plan for that moment of sky: who sits in which section. When you read a chart, you find the seats one by one: first where the Sun sits, then the Moon, then where each of the other planets sits. Reflection prompt A chart records the direction of each body in the sky. This app works out which degree of the zodiac each body sits on — where it appears, seen from Earth. So when you read a chart, what you look at is which section each body sits in. How this tool works What the old texts say: in Book III ch. 2 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy takes the degree rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth as the starting point of the whole chart; everything else is counted from there. Cited source 02Planets are who, signs are the style, houses are which part of life. Three kinds of thing The things on a chart are easier to remember as three kinds: a planet is the who, a sign is the style, a house is which part of life. Take “Sun in Libra”: the Sun is the who, and Libra is its style — talking things out and smoothing things over. Read any row by asking three questions: who? in what style? in which part of life? Later interpretation What sets them apart is whether they need a birth time. Planets and signs come from the date alone (on a day when one changes sign, the candidates are listed). Houses and the Ascendant depend on the Earth's rotation, so they need a birth time to be worked out. With a time, the chart gains the house layer; until a time is entered, that layer stays blank and the planets and signs can still be read. How this tool works What the old texts say: in Book I ch. 4 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy sets out the nature of each of the seven planets one by one. William Lilly's Christian Astrology of 1647, in “Of the Twelve Houses,” names the first house the Ascendant, signifying life and the body. Cited source 03Astrology is a language for describing yourself. Where the claims stop Astrology is a long-lived symbolic language. It gives you words for describing yourself and angles for noticing things. You can hold those words up against your own life and see which fit you and which do not. Reading “Moon in Aries: under pressure, act first, think later,” think back to the last time someone rushed you: did you jump in, or stop and think? Positions are really computed and can be used as they are; readings are handed down and are there to check against yourself. Look at the tag first, then decide how to use the paragraph. LOOK CLOSER

## A common mix-up

A horoscope column usually uses the Sun sign alone — one reading for a twelfth of everybody. A chart is the full table of positions at your moment of birth, and the Sun is just one row of it.

A position on the chart is a direction: which degree of the zodiac each body falls on, seen from Earth. That degree is what the app records.

You can. The date alone places each planet in a sign, and a body that changes sign that day is listed with its candidates. A birth time then adds the Ascendant and house layer.
