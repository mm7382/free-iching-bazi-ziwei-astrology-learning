Your task: go back over your own summary with the checklist.

Table: the Sun's sign is present, with two candidates on a changeover day; most planetary signs are present, with candidates for the fast ones on a boundary day; the Moon often has two candidates; aspects are present and need no birth time; the Ascendant is absent, its row empty; and the twelve houses are absent, the whole layer missing.

Check your summary item by item; each one goes back to an earlier lesson.

## Six things to check

One: did you treat a sign as who you are (“I'm just a Scorpio person”)? Two: did one layer stand in for everything (only the Sun)? Three: did a candidate get written as settled? Four: did you read a layer that is not there (houses with no birth time)? Five: did a category turn into good and bad (good aspects, bad aspects, lucky and unlucky planets)? Six: did a description turn into a prediction (what will happen, whether you will succeed)?

If none of the six apply, your summary broadly holds up. If even one does, go back and reread the lesson it belongs to.

What the old texts say: Book I ch. 2 of Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos says predictions sometimes fail and that seed, place of birth, upbringing and custom must be considered too; Book III ch. 13 says such judgements need many conditions together, never one thing alone.

Each of the six is a shortcut, which is why they are so easy to fall into.

## Why these six are so easy to fall into

The six have one thing in common: each is less work. Calling yourself a sign says it all in one line. Picking one candidate spares you the doubt. Sorting into good and bad spares you working out how things fit together. Writing a prediction spares you admitting you do not know. The honest way is more work at every step; that is its only drawback.

So the checklist is a reminder. You will keep reaching for the shortcut, and that is normal. What matters is whether you have one fixed habit that pulls you back.

Teaching example

## A rewrite, shown

Falls into one and six: “I'm a Capricorn, so I'm bound to succeed.”

Rewritten: “My Saturn is in Capricorn, which tradition reads as building slowly; I do tend to prepare first.”

The difference: it has a subject, a basis, and it speaks about a habit you can already see now.

## A common mix-up

The honest version can be short: a subject, a basis and what you can see now fit in one sentence.

The checklist blocks six known mistakes; whether the content is right goes back to the basis and your real experience. The text itself says predictions sometimes fail.
