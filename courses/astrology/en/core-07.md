THE IDEA

Jupiter and Saturn move slowly; Jupiter shows where you reach out, Saturn where you set limits.

Table: the Moon stays in a sign about two days, the Sun about a month, Mars about two months, Jupiter about a year and Saturn about two and a half years. All averages from NASA orbit periods; retrograde motion changes any single pass.

Jupiter is about reaching out, Saturn about setting limits.

## What the two are about

Jupiter is about reaching out and giving you room: where you are willing to try more and trust a little more. Saturn is about limits, duty and slow build-up: where you hold yourself to the rules and go one step at a time.

The two are easiest to read side by side: one reaches out, one holds in. Learning something new, the Jupiter side wants to sign up for more classes and meet more people, while the Saturn side wants to get the basics solid first.

What the old texts say: in Book I ch. 4 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy says Jupiter’s force is mild because its sphere lies between Saturn’s cooling and Mars’s burning; it both heats and moistens, and so brings winds that help things grow. The same chapter says Saturn mainly cools and dries a little, probably because it is furthest from the Sun’s heat. Ch. 5 counts Jupiter beneficent and Saturn maleficent, on a mild versus extreme nature.

Jupiter changes sign about once a year, Saturn about every two and a half years.

## What slowness means

Jupiter goes round the Sun in about 4,332.589 days (roughly 11.9 years) and Saturn in about 10,755.699 days (roughly 29.5 years), both NASA figures. Shared across twelve signs, Jupiter stays about a year in each and Saturn about two and a half. These are averages too; retrograde motion changes any single stay.

So these two signs are shared by a whole group of people: those born in the same year usually share a Jupiter sign, and those two or three years apart often share a Saturn sign. They describe a backdrop a generation has in common. To read the part that is yours, add the house it fell in, and that needs a birth time.

What the old texts say: Ptolemy writes that Saturn is furthest from the Sun’s heat and that Jupiter’s sphere lies between Saturn’s and Mars’s.

Read them together: where you dare to try, and where you prepare first.

## Reading the pair

A more useful question: where do you let yourself just try things (the Jupiter side)? And where do you feel you must be fully prepared before you start (the Saturn side)? You need both; together they keep things moving. Notice which matters each side covers in your own life.

On your chart, first find the sign each of them is in, then the house. The sign tells you the style of each force, and the house tells you which part of life it goes into; houses need a birth time.

What the old texts say: in Book I ch. 5 of the Tetrabiblos, the labels rest on a mild versus extreme nature; the text also says judgements like this need many conditions weighed together.

Jupiter in Gemini: happy to ask around and try things, gathering first and deciding later.

Both at once: plenty they want to try, but each one rehearsed three times before speaking.

LOOK CLOSER

## A common mix-up

Benefic and malefic mean mild versus extreme in nature. When you read these two, look at where you let yourself try and where you prepare first.

Jupiter changes sign about once a year, so most people born in the same year share one. Reading the personal part takes the houses, and houses need a birth time.

Saturn shows where you make yourself go slowly and prepare first. Things there often start late, but built up step by step they tend to be the most solid.
