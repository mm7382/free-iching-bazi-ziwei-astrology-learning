THE IDEA

A sign is an adjective: it tells you how.

Grouping table: fire is Aries, Leo, Sagittarius; earth is Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn; air is Gemini, Libra, Aquarius; water is Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces. Four groups of three signs each.

Signs move in three ways: starting, holding, shifting.

## Three ways of moving

These three groups are called cardinal, fixed and mutable — think starting, holding, shifting. The starting signs are Aries, Cancer, Libra and Capricorn; the holding signs are Taurus, Leo, Scorpio and Aquarius; the shifting signs are Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius and Pisces. Calling someone “very fixed” means that once they decide, they rarely change.

The three groups answer how someone gets started, keeps going and turns a corner, and each has its own use. The same task can be done with starting energy or with holding energy.

What the old texts say: in Book I ch. 11 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy divides the twelve signs into those at the equinoxes and solstices, the solid ones, and the bicorporeal ones. The split comes from where they sit along the zodiac and how that relates to the turn of the seasons.

The twelve signs fall into four groups: fire, earth, air, water.

## Four element groups

The four groups are fire, earth, air and water, and they describe what a person notices first: fire for drive, earth for the practical, air for information, water for feeling. At the same dinner, fire wants to pick the place, earth minds the budget, air is busy chatting, and water is watching how everyone feels.

Looking at your own chart, how your placements spread across the elements tells you more than any single sign. Count which groups they fall in.

If most placements land in one group, you probably reach for the same approach again and again; if they are spread out, it depends more on the situation. If a group is empty, ask whether that approach is one you seldom use and have to practise on purpose.

What the old texts say: Book I ch. 18 of the Tetrabiblos, “Of the Triangles,” divides the zodiac into four equilateral triangles — the first passing through Aries, Leo and Sagittarius — and sets out which planets govern each. The chapter deals with the familiarity the triangles bring and the planets that rule each one.

A sign is an adjective; it needs a planet to mean anything.

## Use signs as adjectives

Use a sign like an adjective. “Venus in Gemini” reads as “what they like leans toward talking, looking around and comparing”: Gemini describes how Venus shows itself. A sentence needs a planet in it before a sign has anything to describe.

So a planet and a sign together make a sentence with content; a Sun sign by itself is one line shared by a twelfth of everyone. Read your own chart in the same order: name the planet first, then its style — for example “Moon in Aries: feelings act first, think later.”

What the old texts say: Book I ch. 12 of the Tetrabiblos divides the signs into masculine and feminine, taking turns. It is a classification that feeds later rules of judgement.

Sun in Libra (air, equinoctial): opens first and gets the talk going.

Both lean to starting: quick off the mark, but keeping going takes practice.

LOOK CLOSER

## A common mix-up

A chart holds several elements at once, and what you read is how they spread. Someone with a lot of air may often reason things through, but the fire, earth and water in the same chart show up at other times.

Each sign has its own style, with its own strengths and weak spots: starting signs get going fast, holding signs last, shifting signs adjust quickly.

A sign needs a planet to describe: the planet is the subject, and the sign says how it acts.
