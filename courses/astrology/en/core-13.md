THE IDEA

An aspect is how many degrees apart two bodies are; the four main ones are 180, 120, 90 and 60 degrees.

- 180° · 120° · 90° · 60° The four aspect angles, after Ptolemy Book I ch. 13. Diagram: a circle with four chords drawn from the top, for the 180-degree opposition, the 120-degree trine, the 90-degree square and the 60-degree sextile, beside a table saying the same in words. A concept figure, not a real chart. 01The four main aspects: 180, 120, 90 and 60 degrees. The four main aspects An aspect is simply how many degrees apart two positions are — something you can read by measuring an angle. The usual reading treats an aspect as a conversation between two planets: 180 degrees apart is face to face, 90 is catching on each other, 120 and 60 are going along with each other. Later interpretation A quick way to estimate: count how many signs lie between the two. Six apart is roughly an opposition, four a trine, three a square, two a sextile. Sun in Aries and Moon in Leo are four signs apart, so start by guessing a trine. Derived calculation Then check the actual degrees. This app measures the real degrees, and the aspects listed on the chart page are already worked out, so you can check your guess against them. How this tool works What the old texts say: Book I ch. 13 of Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos, “Of the Aspects of the Signs,” is precise — opposition spans two right angles, six signs, 180 degrees; trine one and a third right angles, four signs, 120 degrees; square one right angle, three signs, 90 degrees; sextile two-thirds of a right angle, two signs, 60 degrees. Cited source 02The four aspects cut the circle into halves, thirds, quarters and sixths. Why these four A circle is 360 degrees. 180 is a half, 120 a third, 90 a quarter and 60 a sixth. Held this way, the four aspects are easy to remember, and you can tell which one a figure is at a glance. Derived calculation This app computes exactly these four, plus the conjunction: two bodies bunched together, 0 degrees apart. The chart page lists all five together. How this tool works Astrology today often reads them like this: a conjunction blends two forces; an opposition is a tug of war that asks for balance; a square catches and pushes you to act; a trine runs easily, without effort; a sextile is an opening you have to reach for. Later interpretation What the old texts say: the same chapter gives the reason. Opposition is the obvious one, because the two places sit on one straight line. The rest come from applying the most important musical ratios to the right angle: a half and a third applied to opposition give the square, the sextile and the trine. The text adds that trine and sextile are called harmonious because they join signs of the same kind, all masculine or all feminine, while square and opposition are not. Cited source 03It need not be exactly 120; a table decides how far off still counts. How far off still counts Two bodies are rarely an exact round angle apart, so you need a range for how far off still counts, called the orb. This app uses a common modern table: in a birth chart, 8 degrees for conjunction and opposition, 6 for square and trine, 4 for sextile; transits are read more tightly, 1 degree in general and 2 for the Sun and Moon. Every report records which version of the table it used. How this tool works So if another source shows you different aspects, usually the orbs are set differently. When reading your own chart, start with the aspects closest to exact; they are usually the ones most worth reading first. Reflection prompt What the old texts say: Book I ch. 13 gives exact angles: opposition 180, trine 120, square 90, sextile 60 degrees. Opposition is 180; 2 degrees off is inside the 8-degree orb, so it counts. At 175 degrees — 5 off — it still counts here; 9 off would not. LOOK CLOSER

## A common mix-up

A square is a force that catches and pushes you to act; a trine is one that runs easily. Both are useful. Reading an aspect means asking how smoothly the two forces run together.

In practice you need a range. In a birth chart this app allows 8 degrees for conjunction and opposition, 6 for square and trine and 4 for sextile, and records the version.

Every chart is read the same way: see which two forces each aspect joins, and how they get along.

The orb figures are this app's modern conventional table: in a birth chart 8 degrees for conjunction and opposition, 6 for square and trine, 4 for sextile; 1 degree for transits, 2 for the Sun and Moon. Other sites may use different figures.

An aspect describes how two forces get along; it is not good or bad and says nothing about luck. Astrology is a language for describing yourself, not a forecast.
