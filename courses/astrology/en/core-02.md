THE IDEA

Three placements, three different sides — read them together.

Diagram: an outer ring labelled Sun, an inner ring labelled Moon, and a point at the bottom labelled Ascendant, stacked on one vertical line to show three different layers rather than real degrees.

The Sun is the one people mean when they say “I’m a Leo.”

## Sun

When people say “I’m a Leo,” they mean their Sun sign. The Sun describes where your vitality comes from, and the side of you that shines outward and gets seen; it can also be read as “who you are trying to become.” When you look at your own Sun, ask two things: what am I doing when I feel most alive? What do I most want people to see in me?

The Sun can usually be found from the birth date alone, with no birth time. The Earth takes about 365.26 days to go round the Sun, which works out at roughly one degree a day, and a sign only changes every 30 degrees. So apart from the changeover days, people born on the same date usually have the same Sun sign. That makes the Sun the easiest to look up — and the one most often mistaken for the whole chart.

But if you were born on the day the Sun changed sign and you do not know your birth time, you genuinely have two possible Sun signs. This app lists both, so you can check each one against yourself.

What the old texts say: in Book III ch. 12 of the Tetrabiblos, while dealing with injuries and illness, Ptolemy lists which planet goes with which part of the body and gives the Sun sight, the brain and the heart.

The Moon is about what you need in order to feel settled.

## Moon

The Moon is about your inner needs and habitual reactions: what settles you, where you retreat under pressure, who you are around people you trust. It also explains “I know what to do, yet I still don’t do it”: the Sun wants to go one way while the Moon needs the familiar and the safe, and the two need not agree. Think of the first thing you want to do when you get home after a long day — that is the kind of thing the Moon describes.

The Moon moves fast. It goes round in about 27.32 days, roughly 13 degrees a day, so it changes sign every two days or so. Someone born on a changeover day without a known birth time therefore has two possible Moon signs. This app clearly marks that case as unconfirmed.

When you check this against yourself, notice the gap between what you want to do and what you manage to do. That is more useful than deciding which one is the real you.

What the old texts say: in Book III ch. 13 of the Tetrabiblos, “Of the Quality of the Soul,” Ptolemy gives the parts to do with reason and the mind to Mercury, and the sensory, non-rational part to the Moon. One planet takes thinking, the other feeling — a clear division of labour.

The Ascendant is about the you that people see first.

## Ascendant

The Ascendant is the sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment you were born. It describes the first impression you make and the way you walk into a new room. When friends say “at first I thought you were…, then I got to know you,” the first half is usually what the Ascendant describes.

The Earth turns once in about 23.93 hours, and the twelve signs take turns crossing the eastern horizon — roughly two hours each — so the Ascendant depends entirely on the birth time. No time means no Ascendant and no houses — that whole layer of structure needs a birth time to be laid out. In that case this app shows a clearly labelled demonstration chart so you can learn the idea first.

The Ascendant is like a doorway: people pass through it before they reach the rooms behind. So the impression it gives can differ from the side you most identify with. A mismatch between the two is normal, and both are really you.

What the old texts say: in Book III ch. 2 of the Tetrabiblos, Ptolemy takes the rising degree at the moment of birth as the chart’s starting point. William Lilly’s Christian Astrology of 1647, in “Of the Twelve Houses,” names the first house the Ascendant, signifying life and the body, because it is rising on the eastern horizon.

Teaching example

## A demonstration chart

Sun in Libra: the energy goes into talking things through and smoothing things over.

Moon in Aries: feelings come fast; when uncomfortable, acts first and talks later.

Ascendant in Capricorn: at first sight composed and a little distant — quieter than he really is.

One person, three different statements. The Sun alone suggests someone who goes along with everything; the Ascendant alone suggests someone cold. Read all three together and you get one whole person. · This is a teaching example, not your chart.

LOOK CLOSER

## A common mix-up

The three describe different layers, and all three are you. Reading them together shows more of you than picking one as the real you.

The chart is still useful: the Sun and most planets’ signs hold from the date alone, a birth on a changeover day is listed with both candidates, and a time adds the Ascendant and the houses. This app shows what is certain, marks what is not, and never quietly fills in a time.
