## What is on the chart

A chart is twelve cells around an empty centre. Each cell carries three things: a palace name, a branch and star names. The palace name tells you which part of life the cell is about; the Wealth Palace, for example, is about money coming in and going out. The branch is the cell's fixed position marker; there are twelve of them and they never move. The star names are the stars that fall in that cell; some cells have them and some do not.

## What the text says

The Tai Wei Fu says the stars share a path and the method has its own divisions: a star is read together with the position it sits in. That is the floor of this course: it teaches you to read the chart, and does not judge it for you.

## Compare with your own chart

Open the Chart tab, pick any cell, and read it out in order: the palace name, the branch, and the stars listed. Being able to read it out is enough. If you have no chart yet, practise on the teaching example.

## An everyday example

Think of the chart as a map of life topics. The palace names the topic, the branch marks its address, and stars provide reading cues. A star in the Partner palace is not a label for your partner: first identify the topic you are reading.

Name the palace, branch and star separately first.

On the teaching example, one cell in the right-hand column reads Life Palace; its branch is Xu and the star inside is Po Jun. So the three things in that cell are: the Life Palace, the branch Xu, and the star Po Jun.
