## In plain words

The text says: Tian Ji belongs to Wood and is the third star of the Southern Dipper; Tai Yang belongs to Fire and is the essence of the sun; Tai Yin is the essence of Water and corresponds to the Property Palace. The text does use the images of sun and moon for this pair. These are descriptions, not good or bad.

## What the text says

The lines again come from Volume 1, the questions and answers on the stars. Open the classical basis below to read all three.

## Tian Ji (Strategist Star) · Thinking and adapting

Thinking and adapting. Changing tools can take longer than preparing a proposal. Make one page people can respond to, then revise it using their feedback.

## Tai Yang (Sun Star) · Expression and contribution

Expression and contribution. You might offer a colleague half an hour to clarify a problem rather than taking over the entire task. That provides support while respecting your own schedule.

Define the scope of one offer of help: what you can do and until when.

## Tai Yin (Moon Star) · Reflection and detail

Reflection and detail. A shorter reply can invite many interpretations. Ask whether the person is busy or would prefer to talk later instead of treating a guess as fact.

Write one current feeling and one fact you know. Keep them distinct.

On the example, Tian Ji is in the Friends Palace at Mao, sharing the cell with Ju Men; Tai Yang and Tai Yin are both in the Property Palace at Chou.

- 天機屬木，南斗第三益算之善星也 Tian Ji belongs to Wood. It is the third star of the Southern Dipper, the benevolent star of added reckoning. Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions

- 太陽星屬火，日之精也。乃造化之表儀 The star Tai Yang belongs to Fire. It is the essence of the sun, and is called the outward emblem of creation. Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions

- 太陰乃水之精，為田宅主 Tai Yin is the essence of Water, and is named master of the Tian Zhai (田宅, Property Palace). Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions
