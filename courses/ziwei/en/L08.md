## In plain words

The text says: Tan Lang is the first star of the Northern Dipper and belongs to Water, with an office written as easing hardship; Qi Sha is the sixth star of the Southern Dipper, belongs to Fire and Metal, and is likened to a senior commander; Po Jun belongs to Water and is the seventh star of the Northern Dipper; Lian Zhen belongs to Fire and is the fifth star of the Northern Dipper. The names sound strong, but they are only ancient star names, not statements about who the chart's owner is.

## What the text says

The four lines come from Volume 1, the questions and answers on the stars. Open the classical basis below.

## Compare with your own chart

Find the four stars on your chart. Say only that Po Jun is in such-and-such palace, not that you are a Po Jun type of person.

## Tan Lang (Seeker Star) · Exploration and choice

Exploration and choice. Several invitations may all appeal. Choosing one to engage with fully can be more meaningful than rushing between them.

List three things you want to try and schedule just one this week.

## Qi Sha (Warrior Star) · Decisions and responsibility

Decisions and responsibility. Changing direction can unblock a project while leaving colleagues confused. Explain the reason, constraints and next step so the decision can be carried out together.

Before an important decision, distinguish reversible from irreversible consequences.

## Po Jun (Vanguard Star) · Renewal and trade-offs

Renewal and trade-offs. Rebuilding an entire routine at once can be difficult to sustain. Try changing one evening habit for a week before deciding what else to change.

For one habit, write what to retain, what to stop and what to test first.

## Lian Zhen (Principle Star) · Boundaries and commitment

Boundaries and commitment. A late message may feel like an obligation even when no immediate reply was agreed. Explain when you are available rather than letting resentment build.

Choose a recurring uncomfortable situation and express one clear boundary.

On the example, Po Jun is in the Life Palace at Xu, Qi Sha in the Wealth Palace at Wu, Tan Lang in the Career Palace at Yin, and Lian Zhen in the Partner Palace at Shen. These are just positions; every chart has these four stars.

- 貪狼北斗解厄之神，第一星也。屬水 Tan Lang is the Northern Dipper's deity that dissolves adversity, and its first star. It belongs to Water. Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions

- 七殺南斗第六星也，屬火、金。乃斗中之上將 Qi Sha is the sixth star of the Southern Dipper and belongs to Fire and Metal. It is called the senior commander within the Dipper. Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions

- 破軍屬水，北斗第七星也，司夫妻、子息、奴僕之神 Po Jun belongs to Water and is the seventh star of the Northern Dipper, the deity said to govern spouse, offspring and attendants. Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions

- 廉貞屬火，北斗第五星也。在斗司品秩，在數司權令 Lian Zhen belongs to Fire and is the fifth star of the Northern Dipper. In the Dipper it is said to govern rank and order; in the method it is said to govern authority and command. Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions
