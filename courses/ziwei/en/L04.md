## In plain words

Reading has three layers. First, chart facts: which palace, which branch, which stars are listed; they are worked out and can be checked. Second, classical sources: which book and volume, and what the text says. Third, interpretation: this project's plain wording and the views of different schools. Facts come first, then sources, then interpretation; an interpretation never overrides a fact.

## What the text says

Volume 3 sets out an order: first the body and life markers, then the wealth, career and travel palaces. The text itself cares about what comes first; this course keeps that habit of reading in order.

## Compare with your own chart

Every passage in this app is labelled: classical or rule basis, plain explanation, or this project's own writing. Check the label first and you know whether the passage is a fact, a source or an interpretation.

## An everyday example

When you read that a star suggests communication skills, ask whether the statement is chart data, a quotation or modern commentary. Positions can be checked; interpretations need context and comparison with experience.

On the teaching example the Life Palace is at Xu with Po Jun: that is a fact. That Po Jun belongs to Water and is the seventh star of the Northern Dipper is what Volume 1 says. If someone turns that into a personality, that is an interpretation, and you should ask whose it is.
