## In plain words

The text says: Wu Qu is the sixth star of the Northern Dipper, belongs to Metal and corresponds to the Wealth Palace; Tian Tong belongs to Water, is the fourth southern star and corresponds to the Inner Life Palace; Tian Liang belongs to Earth, is the second star of the Southern Dipper and governs lifespan; Ju Men belongs to Water and Metal and is the second star of the Northern Dipper. Two of them are tied directly to a palace; that is a pairing of star and palace, not good or bad.

## What the text says

All four lines come from Volume 1, the questions and answers on the stars. Open the classical basis below.

## Compare with your own chart

Find which palace each of the four is in. Ju Men sometimes shares a cell with another major star.

## Wu Qu (General Star) · Execution and resources

Execution and resources. Paying bills and doing chores matter, but a partner may also need acknowledgement. Practical contribution and feeling understood both deserve attention.

Break one goal into a step you can finish today and state the support you need.

## Tian Tong (Peacemaker Star) · Ease and connection

Ease and connection. Saying 'anything is fine' when choosing a restaurant may hide a real preference. Try expressing one need while remaining open to the group's options.

Replace one 'anything is fine' with a specific, kindly expressed preference.

## Tian Liang (Elder Star) · Care and principles

Care and principles. A family member venting about work may not want a plan immediately. Ask whether they would prefer company or help thinking through options.

Before offering advice, ask what kind of help the person wants.

## Ju Men (Gate Star) · Questions and clarity

Questions and clarity. Instead of accusing someone of being vague about a deadline, ask whether they mean Wednesday before the end of the day. Specific questions produce usable answers.

Rephrase one accusatory question to clarify timing, scope or needs.

On the example, Wu Qu is in the Inner Life Palace at Zi, sharing the cell with Tian Fu; Tian Tong is in the Parents Palace at Hai; Tian Liang is in the Health Palace at Si; Ju Men is in the Friends Palace at Mao with Tian Ji. The text pairs Wu Qu with Wealth, yet here it is not in the Wealth Palace: a pairing does not mean the star sits there.

- 武曲北斗第六星，屬金，乃財帛宮主 Wu Qu is the sixth star of the Northern Dipper. It belongs to Metal and is named master of the Cai Bo (財帛, Wealth Palace). Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions

- 天同星屬水，乃南方第四星也，為福德宮之主宰 The star Tian Tong belongs to Water. It is the fourth star of the south, and is named master of the Fu De (福德, Inner Life Palace). Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions

- 天梁屬土，南斗第二星也。司壽化氣為蔭 Tian Liang belongs to Earth and is the second star of the Southern Dipper. It is said to govern lifespan, and its transformed influence is called shelter. Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions

- 巨門屬水、金。北斗第二星也，為陰精之星 Ju Men belongs to Water and Metal. It is the second star of the Northern Dipper, and is called the star of yin essence. Zi Wei Dou Shu Quan Shu (紫微斗數全書), Volume 1, "Questions and Answers on the Stars" (諸星問答論); traditionally attributed in received editions
