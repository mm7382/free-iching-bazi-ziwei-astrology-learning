64 Hexagrams / 54

Where standing is unclear, enthusiasm alone tends to fail.

## Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Gui Mei: Advancing brings misfortune. Nothing is beneficial.

The Tuan commentary affirms the union of man and woman as the great meaning of heaven and earth and the end and beginning of human affairs, then names the flaw directly: acting out of delight, in a position that does not suit, with the yielding riding above the firm, so going forward brings misfortune and nothing is favourable. The Xiang image is thunder above the lake, and the noble person thinks through the lasting end and knows where it may fail.

Zhen above, Dui below. Thunder rolls over the lake: a younger woman marries into a place where her standing is not right. The hexagram describes a union whose conditions are inadequate from the start. The lame manage to walk, the half-sighted manage to see, the third line waits for a proper position and settles for a secondary one, the fourth simply delays the wedding, the fifth outshone by her own attendants, and the last closes with an empty basket and a sacrifice that draws no blood. The judgement is blunt. The image offers the way out: think the ending and the failure modes through at the beginning.

The judgment's misfortune in advancing and nothing beneficial form a rare double negative. Its commentary attributes this to inappropriate positions and yielding above firmness. The top ends with an empty basket and a slaughtered sheep without blood, an image of total emptiness. Positive terms occur only in the first nine and fifth six, both conditioned on accepting a secondary place without competing over appearances; the second also favors quiet steadfastness. Structural inequality rather than a passing mistake creates the unfavorable factors, hence the rating: Poor. This is the app's interpretation of the text, not a guarantee of outcomes.

Worth doing: clarify the standing, the responsibilities and the long-term cost before deciding whether to commit. Avoid: being carried in by enthusiasm, or expecting an unequal arrangement to improve by itself.

## Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Zhen above, Dui below: pleasure underneath, movement above — a structure of unequal positions.

- Name the upper and lower trigram of Gui Mei.

- Contrast Gui Mei with Sui.

- Say that swapping the halves turns The Marrying Maiden into Following.

The lower trigram of Gui Mei is Dui: two solid lines with a broken third. The upper is Zhen: a solid fourth line with two broken above. The first, second and fourth lines are solid. It uses the same two trigrams as Sui, in the other order.

In Sui movement is below and pleasure above: what is below moves first and what is above goes along. Gui Mei puts pleasure below and movement above, so the lower side accommodates while the upper moves on its own. Structurally this reads as unequal position: the thing may well happen, but not on terms you set. Tradition discusses it through marriage, and the point is entering an arrangement someone else already made.

A teaching example, not a prediction: a small team joins an existing large programme whose standards and schedule were set by others. Gui Mei describes that structure; it does not say the collaboration fails.

## The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

### Nine at the beginning: The maiden marries as a secondary wife. The lame can walk. Advancing brings good fortune.

The Xiang commentary credits marrying as the secondary bride with constancy, and says the lame one walks because each supports the other rather than one straining alone.

You enter in the secondary role of the attending bride. It is awkward, like walking with a lame foot, yet you can still walk, and going forward is favourable. The commentary puts the key in persistence and mutual support.

Joining as a deputy or a stand-in can still amount to something, provided you accept the position and stay in it steadily. Grabbing for the lead role instead leaves you with no footing at all.

If this role did not get upgraded within a year, would you still do it well?

### Nine in the second place: The one-eyed can see. A secluded person's steadfastness is beneficial.

The Xiang commentary says this favours the steadfastness of one who lives quietly, because the constant way has not been altered. The circumstances are limited, the conduct is not.

Like someone blind in one eye who can still see, you see, but not the whole. This suits a person content with quiet who holds correct, because circumstances do not shift their principles.

When your information is incomplete and your say is limited, making large judgements is risky. Quietly doing well the part you can see clearly, and keeping your principles, is safer than rushing to declare a view.

How much of this do you actually see clearly?

### Six in the third place: The maiden waits to marry, then returns as a secondary wife.

The Xiang commentary says waiting to marry in this way is not suitable to the position. The expectation does not match the circumstances, so the return is to the secondary role after all.

You had hoped to wait for a better standing before marrying, the wait yielded nothing, and you enter in the secondary role regardless. The commentary locates the problem in an expectation out of proportion to your situation.

Setting your terms above what you can currently trade for tends to land you back where you started, with the waiting time spent for nothing. Assess your actual hand honestly, then decide whether to wait or accept.

Is the condition you are holding out for in proportion to what you can offer now?

### Nine in the fourth place: The maiden's marriage is delayed. A late marriage has its proper time.

The Xiang commentary says the intent behind the delay is to wait and then act. This is not procrastination but a deliberate hold for the fitting moment.

The set date passes and the marriage has not taken place; a later going has its own time. The commentary reads this as intentional waiting rather than drift.

Moving the schedule back yourself when the conditions are not ready is a responsible choice, not a failure. Spell out why you are delaying and what condition restarts it, and others accept it more readily.

What condition has to appear before you restart the thing you postponed?

### Six in the fifth place: Di Yi gives the maiden in marriage. Her sleeves are less fine than her secondary wife's. The moon is nearly full. Good fortune.

The Xiang commentary notes the sovereign's daughter whose sleeves are plainer than her attendant's, and says she holds the central position and moves by the dignity of her character rather than competing through dress. The moon is nearly full, not yet full.

The sovereign marries off his daughter and her sleeves are less fine than her attendant's. The moon is nearly full but not quite, and the line reads well. Her standing rests on the centred character, not the appearance.

People with genuine weight do not need to outshine those around them, and giving away the spotlight tends to earn more respect. Leaving something in reserve is also safer than using everything up.

Where recently could you have let go of the most visible position?

### Six at the top: The woman holds an empty basket; the man slaughters a sheep without blood. Nothing is beneficial.

The Xiang commentary says the top line has no substance and carries an empty basket. The rite is outwardly complete and inwardly hollow.

The woman carries a basket with nothing in it and the man cuts the sheep without drawing blood. What should have been substance is entirely absent, and no advantage is visible here.

A relationship or partnership where the ritual is complete and the substance is empty does not hold. Rather than staging a grander ceremony, admit the content is missing and renegotiate.

In this arrangement, which is fuller, the form or the substance?

## Quick Quiz

- The same Dui and Zhen with their positions reversed

- They differ by one line

- They are the same

**The same Dui and Zhen trigrams, with upper and lower positions reversed (Correct)** : Correct. Sui has Zhen below and Dui above; Gui Mei has Dui below and Zhen above.

**They differ by one line** : They differ in the positions of entire trigrams.

**They are the same** : The solid lines occupy different positions.

- It may well happen, but not on terms you set

- It cannot work out

- The two sides are fully equal

**The undertaking will take shape, but you do not set its terms (Correct)** : Correct. Joy below and movement above place you in a supporting position.

**It cannot work out** : The hexagram does not state failure.

**The two sides are fully equal** : Unequal positions are precisely this hexagram's focus.

- Zhen (one solid below, two broken above)

- Dui · Lake (two solid lines below, one broken above)

- Kun · Earth (three broken lines)

**Zhen · Thunder (one solid line below, two broken above) (Correct)** : Correct. The fourth line is solid; the fifth and sixth broken.

**Dui · Lake (two solid lines below, one broken above)** : Dui is Gui Mei's lower trigram.

**Kun · Earth (three broken lines)** : The fourth line is solid.
