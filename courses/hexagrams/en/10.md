Proceed carefully, observe propriety and restraint, and advance steadily toward success

## Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Lu: Treading on the tiger's tail, it does not bite the person. Success.

Commentary on the Judgment: “Treading: the yielding treads upon the firm. Joyfully responding to Qian, one treads on the tiger's tail without being bitten. Success. Firm, central, and correct, one treads the ruler's position without fault: this is brightness.” Commentary on the Image: “Heaven above, lake below: Treading. The noble person distinguishes higher and lower positions and settles the people's purpose.” (Zhouyi, Commentaries on the Judgment and the Image; public domain)

Lu depicts Heaven high above and a lake below: stepping on a tiger's tail without being bitten. Careful, courteous conduct can bring progress even in danger. Qian is above and Dui below, meeting Heaven's firmness with a joyful heart. This hexagram emphasizes propriety and clear distinctions of position. It calls for steady progress through a tense environment with judgment, sincerity, and respect, rather than fearful retreat.

Lu's judgment explicitly states “success”: careful, courteous action can progress. Its overall direction is positive, although the third line warns against recklessness. Good fortune is associated with care and restraint, hence the rating: Highly Favorable.

Do: Check the rules and appropriate limits, watching the other person's responses at each step. Avoid: Crossing boundaries on the strength of luck or relaxing courtesy after brief success.

## Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Qian above, Dui below: the only broken line is the third, exactly at the boundary between the two trigrams.

- Identify Lu's upper and lower trigrams.

- Explain why the broken line's position matters.

- Work out that exchanging Lu's trigrams produces Guai · Breakthrough.

Lu's lower trigram is Dui: two solid lines below and a broken line at the top. Its upper trigram is Qian, with three solid lines. The hexagram's only broken line is the third, at the top of the lower trigram—the meeting point of the two trigrams.

Five solid lines provide abundant firmness, but the only yielding line stands at the boundary. This position is read as “treading”: the step beneath you is soft, with the entire Qian trigram pressing above. The reminder concerns your footing rather than your ability: sufficient strength does not guarantee a stable step.

Teaching example, not a prediction: A plan has everything in place; the only uncertainty is who handles the handover. Capability is not the issue; the boundary is. Lu describes this structure.

## The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

### Nine at the beginning: Simple conduct. Proceed; no blame.

Commentary on the Image: “Advancing with simple conduct means independently following one's purpose.” Moving simply and genuinely means following your own wish independently. The first nine advances with simplicity and integrity, naturally incurring no blame. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Move forward simply and honestly, relying on your true nature rather than packaging or tactics, and you naturally avoid blame.

No extra embellishment or strategy is needed. Act simply and genuinely; with the right direction, simplicity itself is your best passport.

Are you overpackaging yourself or thinking too strategically anywhere? How might things differ if you returned to your simplest, truest self?

### Nine in the second place: Treading a level, open path. The secluded person's steadfastness brings good fortune.

Commentary on the Image: “The secluded person's steadfastness brings good fortune, because their center is not disturbed.” A recluse who remains correct has good fortune because outside things do not unsettle their centeredness. The second nine occupies the center and follows a level road with quiet balance. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

The road is level and open. Even someone who chooses a quiet, understated life can find good fortune by remaining true. Advance with a balanced heart, undisturbed by outside noise.

If you choose to live quietly rather than follow the crowd, staying on your own right path can make that quiet life a source of good fortune in itself.

Are you living to meet external expectations rather than proceeding in a way that truly suits you? Hold your center, and the road becomes more open.

### Six in the third place: The one-eyed can see; the lame can walk. Treading on the tiger's tail, one is bitten. Misfortune. A warrior acts as the great ruler.

Commentary on the Image: “The one-eyed can see, but not clearly enough. The lame can walk, but not well enough to keep pace. Being bitten brings misfortune because the position is inappropriate. The warrior acts as the great ruler because his purpose is forceful.” Believing they can see clearly or walk normally despite limitations, they overestimate their abilities and enter danger; their inappropriate position brings misfortune. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Forcing action despite significant limitations, one overestimates one's abilities and steps onto the tiger's tail—not merely bitten, but facing misfortune. This is the cost of failing to recognize one's limits clearly.

Forcing yourself into a role beyond your abilities when your skills or conditions are insufficient often brings danger. Honestly assess your current situation.

Are you forcing yourself to take on something beyond your present abilities? Is this coming from outside pressure or a lack of honesty with yourself?

### Nine in the fourth place: Treading on the tiger's tail, trembling with fear. Good fortune in the end.

Commentary on the Image: “Trembling, yet fortunate in the end, because one's purpose is carried out.” Though close to the tiger's tail, the fourth nine acts with caution and fear, ultimately finding good fortune as its purpose is fulfilled. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Treading on the tiger's tail, you tremble with caution. Yet that very care brings good fortune in the end. Caution itself offers protection.

In a high-risk situation, a cautious, watchful attitude can serve as armor. Do not let nervousness overwhelm you; let it make you more careful.

Are you currently navigating something very carefully? Does that caution help you advance or make you retreat?

### Nine in the fifth place: Resolute treading. Steadfastness brings danger.

Commentary on the Image: “Resolute treading; steadfastness brings danger, because the position is properly occupied.” Decisive action carries risk because the role bears great responsibility. The fifth nine holds a high and correct position, yet its heavy responsibility means potential dangers cannot be ignored. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Decisive action from a high position may be correct yet still dangerous. The more pivotal your position, the more vigilance you need. Being right does not remove the need for care.

Your decision may be right, but the more important your position, the more carefully you must consider its consequences. Correctness does not equal safety; responsibility matters more.

Are you overlooking potential risks or effects on others because you believe your current decision is right?

### Nine at the top: Examine your conduct and assess its signs. Returning through the full cycle brings supreme good fortune.

Commentary on the Image: “Supreme good fortune at the top means there is much to celebrate.” The top nine reviews its conduct along the way and examines its auspiciousness. Completing the cycle brings great good fortune and celebration. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

At the end, look back at your actions and examine whether they followed a right and auspicious path. Completing the full circle brings the greatest good fortune and celebration.

At the end of a stage, take time to review your path and whether it aligns with your core values. That review itself brings substantial good fortune.

If you look back now at your recent choices and actions, do you see the person you truly wanted to be?

## Quick Quiz

- Dui · Lake (two solid lines below, one broken above)

- Xun · Wind (one broken line below, two solid above)

- Qian · Heaven (three solid lines)

**Dui · Lake (two solid lines below, one broken above) (Correct)** : Correct. Solid first and second lines with a broken third line form Dui.

**Xun · Wind (one broken line below, two solid above)** : Xun's broken line is at the bottom. Lu's first line is solid.

**Qian · Heaven (three solid lines)** : Qian is the upper trigram in Lu.

- It is the top of the lower trigram, at the boundary with the upper trigram

- It is the center of the whole hexagram, so it is most important

- It must be a yin line

**It is the top of the lower trigram, at the boundary with the upper trigram (Correct)** : Correct. The seam between the two trigrams lies between the third and fourth lines.

**It is the center of the whole hexagram, so it is most important** : Traditionally, central positions refer to the second and fifth lines, each at the center of a trigram.

**It must be a yin line** : The third line can be yin or yang. Qian's third line is solid.

- Guai · Breakthrough

- Xiao Xu · Small Accumulation

- Tong Ren · Fellowship

**Guai · Breakthrough (Correct)** : Correct. Qian below and Dui above form Hexagram 43, Guai · Breakthrough. The broken line moves from the third to the sixth position.

**Xiao Xu · Small Accumulation** : Xiao Xu has Qian below and Xun above, with a broken fourth line.

**Tong Ren · Fellowship** : Tong Ren has Li below and Qian above, with a broken line in the middle of its lower trigram.
