## Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Jia Ren: A woman's steadfastness is beneficial.

The Tuan commentary describes each member holding their proper place, and calls an ordered household the starting point for an ordered world: when parent is parent, child is child and each role is filled, the way of the family is correct. The Xiang image is wind issuing from fire, with the noble person making their words carry substance and their conduct hold to a constant standard.

Li below, Xun above: wind rising out of fire, a hexagram about order and division of work inside a group. The judgement speaks in its own period's terms of the one who keeps the interior holding correct, and the core of it is that every member stands in their own place and does that part well. The lines begin by guarding against disorder from the very start, pass through the two failures of excessive severity and unchecked levity, then through prosperity and mutual affection, and end with sincerity and a bearing that commands respect through self-examination. What holds a household together is a long-standing standard and the example you set, not a burst of warmth.

The judgment's beneficial steadfastness for a woman is positive, as are great good fortune in the fourth six, good fortune in the fifth nine, and eventual good fortune at the top. Its commentary grounds order in the world in a rightly ordered family. Yet the third nine warns that unrestrained merriment among wife and children ends in regret. Favorability depends on proper limits, hence the rating: Good, rather than the highest. This is the app's interpretation of the text, not a guarantee of outcomes.

Worth doing: state the rules clearly, keep to them over time, and set the example from your own role first. Avoid: demanding compliance only from the people closest to you, or letting mood take the place of a household boundary.

## Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Xun above, Li below: brightness within, wind without — a structure that works outward.

- Name the upper and lower trigram of Jia Ren.

- Explain why it is used to discuss a household.

- Work out that The Household with its halves swapped is The Cauldron.

The lower trigram of Jia Ren is Li: solid, broken, solid. The upper is Xun: a broken fourth line with two solid above. The first, third, fifth and sixth lines are solid.

Li is inside and Xun outside: brightness within, going along without. The hexagram is used for households not because of its name but because the structure says that things go smoothly outside only once they are clear inside. The order cannot be reversed: if the inside is unclear, outward smoothness is only accommodation.

A teaching example, not a prediction: a team first settles its internal division of work and standards, and only then finds external communication easy. Jia Ren describes that structure working from inside out.

## The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

### Nine at the beginning: Establish boundaries in the household. Regret vanishes.

The Xiang commentary says that setting limits for the household at the outset works because the will has not yet shifted. Guarding against trouble in advance is what clears the regret.

You establish boundaries and rules for the household at the very beginning, and the regret dissolves. The commentary stresses that the moment to set a standard is before people's habits have drifted, because fixing it afterwards is much harder.

For a new team, a new shared home or a new partnership, the cheapest approach is to state the rules in the first week rather than patching them after a conflict.

That rule you keep avoiding, does it not get harder to raise the longer you wait?

### Six in the second place: Pursue nothing independently; attend to nourishment within. Steadfastness brings good fortune.

The Xiang commentary says the good fortune here comes from yielding and modesty. Seeking no achievement outward and attending to the provisions within, one rests in the proper place and holds correct.

You are not pushing for outward accomplishment. You take good care of the daily business in front of you, and holding correct goes well. The value lies not in the scale of what you do but in whether your own part is genuinely done.

There is no need to chase new projects or make yourself visible just now. Doing the routine, unwatched work reliably is what builds people's reasons to trust you.

The most unremarkable routine task on your list, are you doing it well enough?

### Nine in the third place: Sternness among family members: regret and danger, yet good fortune. Wife and children laughing without restraint: regret in the end.

The Xiang commentary says that stern voices in the household have not lost the way, while unchecked laughter among family members loses the measure of the house, which is why it ends in regret.

Running a household too strictly brings regret and some danger, yet it still ends well. Letting it slide into everyone laughing without limit ends in nothing but regret. Between the two, err on the side of too strict rather than losing the measure altogether.

Holding people tightly makes them uncomfortable, but saying yes to everything eventually leaves no standard at all. When you have to choose, leaning towards saying it plainly is usually the safer side.

Is your recent leniency kindness, or avoidance of a difficult conversation?

### Six in the fourth place: Enriching the household. Great good fortune.

The Xiang commentary attributes the great good fortune of a prosperous household to yielding while holding the right position. This abundance is not a matter of luck.

The household is well provided for and the reading is strongly favourable. The commentary points to resting in the proper place and acting in accordance with reason, which is how the abundance was built up rather than seized.

In the stage where resources start to loosen up, keep doing the things that got you here. Do not change your rhythm just because there is more room.

How many of the habits that produced this abundance are you still keeping?

### Nine in the fifth place: The king approaches his household. Do not worry. Good fortune.

The Xiang commentary says that when the ruler reaches the household, the result is mutual affection between all. Reaching here means moving others by virtue, which is why there is nothing to worry about.

The one in the senior position moves the whole household by character rather than pressure, so there is nothing to be anxious about and the line goes well. The commentary calls it mutual affection: an order people enter willingly.

When things get done well without you watching, trust has been established. What that moment calls for is continuing to set the example, not increasing supervision.

Does your influence rest on your authority or on people's willingness?

### Nine at the top: With sincerity and dignity, good fortune in the end.

The Xiang commentary says the good fortune of a commanding presence comes from turning back on oneself. Sincerity is full and the bearing is worthy of respect, because the demand is made of oneself first.

You stand on sincerity and carry yourself in a way that earns respect, and in the end it goes well. The commentary is blunt about the mechanism: real authority comes from holding yourself to it first, not from raising your voice at others.

The most effective way to get people to keep a rule is to keep it yourself. What you actually do carries far more weight than what you have said.

The standard you ask of others, have you met it yourself?
