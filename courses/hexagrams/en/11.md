64 Hexagrams / 11

Heaven and Earth meet in harmony; communication flows between higher and lower levels. Use favorable timing to move forward actively.

## Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Tai: The small departs, the great arrives. Good fortune and success.

Commentary on the Judgment: “In Tai, the small departs and the great arrives: good fortune and success. Heaven and Earth meet, and all things communicate; higher and lower meet and share one purpose. Yang within and yin without, strength within and receptiveness without, noble people within and petty people without: the noble way grows and the petty way declines.” Commentary on the Image: “Heaven and Earth meet: Peace. The ruler shapes and completes their way, assists what is fitting to them, and thereby guides and supports the people.” (Zhouyi, Commentaries on the Judgment and the Image; public domain)

Tai symbolizes Heaven and Earth joining so that all things flow freely: Qian below rises while Kun above descends, allowing full exchange between yin and yang. This is the most open overall state, with vigorous yang within and receptive cooperation outside. The noble way grows as the petty way recedes. The Image reminds the wise ruler to use this harmony to order Heaven and Earth's processes and assist all things for people's benefit, rather than simply enjoy favorable conditions and neglect governance.

Tai says, “The small departs, the great arrives. Good fortune and success.” Heaven and Earth communicate and all things flow. One of the most open and auspicious states among the 64 hexagrams, it has an extremely positive overall direction, hence the rating: Highest Favorability.

Do: Use an opening for smooth communication, coordinate higher and lower levels, and advance important work. Avoid: Enjoying good conditions while ignoring risk, or claiming all credit yourself.

## Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Kun above, Qian below: these same two trigrams in this arrangement form Tai.

- Identify Tai's upper and lower trigrams.

- Explain why changing the positions changes the hexagram.

- Clearly explain the structural difference between Tai and Pi.

Tai stacks two trigrams: Qian below, with three solid lines, and Kun above, with three broken lines. Counted upward, the first three lines are yang and the last three yin. Hexagrams are always read from bottom to top, so “Qian below, Kun above” describes the same diagram.

Qian represents Heaven and upward force; Kun represents Earth, support, and downward movement. Tai puts Qian below and Kun above, so the forces move toward each other and meet. Traditionally, this structure is described as “open communication.” This does not promise smooth outcomes; it describes an arrangement in which exchange is possible.

Teaching example, not a prediction: In a team, managers listen first and delegate authority downward, while frontline staff share actual conditions upward. Positions may seem reversed, yet information flows. Tai describes this structure; it does not mean the team is certain to succeed.

## The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

### Nine at the beginning: Pulling up thatch with its connected roots, others of its kind follow. Advancing brings good fortune.

Commentary on the Image: “Pulling up thatch and advancing brings good fortune, because the purpose reaches outward.” Pulling thatch draws up connected plants with it. Advancing is fortunate because the aim is outward expansion. The first nine is firm below; those of its kind gather and move outward together, bringing good fortune. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Like thatch pulled up with intertwined roots, kindred people follow one another. Moving outward together brings good fortune. Like-minded people acting together multiply their effectiveness.

This is an auspicious time for active movement. Find like-minded companions and move forward together; a shared purpose multiplies your strength.

Do you have like-minded partners who can advance the plan with you, or are you used to working alone?

### Nine in the second place: Embrace the uncultivated. Wade across the river; do not neglect the distant. Let factions fall away. Be honored for walking the middle path.

Commentary on the Image: “Embracing the uncultivated and being honored for the middle path brings breadth and illumination.” Inclusive action through the middle way leads to greatness. The second nine occupies the center and acts with broad-mindedness, without factionalism or neglect of distant concerns. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Embrace the uncultivated and cross a great river without concern for personal safety. Do not overlook distant affairs or form factions. Act through the middle way to achieve breadth and illumination.

In favorable times, maintain an especially inclusive mind. Avoid favoritism, do not overlook people or matters at the margins, and guide the whole with balance and integrity.

Do favorable conditions draw you into cliques or favoritism? Are there important people or matters you are overlooking?

### Nine in the third place: No level ground without a slope; no departure without return. Steadfastness through hardship brings no blame. Do not worry about trustworthiness; there is blessing in sustenance.

Commentary on the Image: “No departure without return: this is the meeting of Heaven and Earth.” Going and returning follow the law of their meeting. The third nine stands at the end of Peace's lower trigram, warning that a peak gives way to decline. Remaining steadfast in hardship still brings no blame. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

No ground stays level forever, and nothing only departs without returning. Favorable conditions eventually fluctuate. Remain correct in hardship and there is no blame, with blessing in your sustenance; do not worry excessively.

Do not assume good times will last forever. Stay ready for change. Those who hold to right conduct in hardship can still gain tangible benefits.

Are you preparing for possible adversity even while things go well, or are you entirely absorbed in the present favorable state?

### Six in the fourth place: Fluttering together, not wealthy, with one's neighbors; trusting without admonition.

Commentary on the Image: “Fluttering without wealth, all have lost substance. Trusting without admonition comes from the heart's wish.” Lightly gathering without wealth reflects a shared loss of substance; trust without reminders arises from sincere inner willingness. The fourth six approaches Peace's end, relating through trust without needing pressure. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Gathering lightly, neighbors are held together by sincere trust rather than wealth. Believing one another without admonition is the most sincere kind of relationship.

The best relationships need neither wealth nor interests to bind them, nor constant reminders of loyalty. Sincerity from the heart is itself the strongest connection.

Do your important relationships depend partly on benefits or promises? Are there parts sustained purely by mutual trust?

### Six in the fifth place: Di Yi gives his daughter in marriage. Through blessing, supreme good fortune.

Commentary on the Image: “Blessing and supreme good fortune arise from fulfilling the heart's wish from the center.” Di Yi marries off his daughter with humble propriety, an image of blessing that brings a good undertaking to fruition. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Like a king humbly marrying his daughter to someone of lower rank, sincere blessing brings a good undertaking to fruition. Refusing to boast of high status brings the greatest good fortune instead.

If you hold an advantageous position, humbly helping others succeed with sincere goodwill and setting status aside brings the greatest good fortune through that humility itself.

Do you have an opportunity to help someone of lower status with humility? What stands in the way?

### Six at the top: The city wall falls back into the moat. Do not use the army. Proclaim commands from your own town. Steadfastness brings regret.

Commentary on the Image: “The wall falls back into the moat because commands are disordered.” Peace reaches its height and turns to Stagnation; the wall collapses. This is no time for warfare. Begin by restoring order and issuing reminders within your own core. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

At the peak, decline sets in and the wall falls into the moat. Do not turn military force outward; put your own affairs in order first. Even steadfastness carries regret because the larger situation has changed.

When favorable conditions end, do not try to sustain them through ever greater action. Start by putting yourself and your core affairs in order. Accept and adapt to the change rather than force things.

Do you sense cracks appearing in a situation that once flowed smoothly? Are you trying to preserve it with greater force, or are you ready to change course?

## Quick Quiz

- Qian · Heaven (three solid lines)

- Kun · Earth (three broken lines)

- Li · Fire (solid top and bottom, broken middle)

**Qian · Heaven (three solid lines) (Correct)** : Correct. The first three lines counted upward are yang, forming Qian.

**Kun · Earth (three broken lines)** : Kun is Tai's upper trigram, not its lower. The first three lines from the bottom form solid-lined Qian.

**Li · Fire (solid top and bottom, broken middle)** : Li's middle line is broken, while Tai's lower three lines are all solid.

- Pi · Stagnation

- Qian · The Creative

- Still Tai

**Pi · Stagnation (Correct)** : Correct. Moving Qian above and Kun below produces Pi.

**Qian · The Creative** : Qian has six yang lines. Exchanging trigrams changes positions, not the number of yin and yang lines. The answer is Pi.

**Still Tai** : When the two trigrams differ, exchanging them always produces another hexagram. Here it produces Pi.

- The same components in opposite upper and lower positions

- One line changes from yin to yang or vice versa

- Only the names differ; the diagrams are identical

**The same components in opposite upper and lower positions (Correct)** : Correct. Both consist of Qian and Kun; the only difference is which is above.

**One line changes from yin to yang or vice versa** : That describes a changing line, with one line changing at a time. Tai and Pi differ in the positions of whole trigrams; all six lines change position.

**Only the names differ; the diagrams are identical** : The diagrams differ: Tai has three solid lines below and three broken above; Pi is the reverse.
