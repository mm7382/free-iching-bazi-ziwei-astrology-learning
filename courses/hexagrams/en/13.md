## Judgment and Plain-Language Notes

Tong Ren: Fellowship in the open fields. Success. It is beneficial to cross the great river and beneficial for the noble person to remain steadfast.

Commentary on the Judgment: “Fellowship: the yielding attains its proper and central place and responds to Qian. Thus it is called Fellowship. Fellowship in the open fields brings success. Crossing the great river is beneficial: this is Qian's action. Clarity joins strength; central correctness responds, embodying the noble person's right conduct. Only the noble person can understand and connect the purposes of all under Heaven.” Commentary on the Image: “Heaven together with fire: Fellowship. The noble person groups people by kind and distinguishes things.” (Zhouyi, Commentaries on the Judgment and the Image; public domain)

Tong Ren has Qian above and Li below. Heaven and fire respond to each other, symbolizing people of different backgrounds connected through a common goal. The judgment places their gathering in an open field, reminding us not to confine cooperation to a close circle. Recognizing differences and maintaining right conduct establish a basis for facing difficulties together. The six lines also show exclusion, suspicion, and conflict: fellowship is not automatic harmony, but requires public principles and ongoing communication.

The judgment's “success” and “beneficial to cross the great river” favor cooperation and shared responsibility, hence the rating: Highly Favorable. The second line's regret, third line's defensiveness, and fourth line's confrontation still remind us that results depend on overcoming self-interest. This rating is the app's interpretation of the text, not a guarantee of outcomes.

Do: Gather partners around a common purpose, make rules public, and accommodate differences. Avoid: Alliances limited to familiar people or treating disagreement as hostility.

## Understand This Hexagram Through Its Structure

Qian above, Li below: the only broken line is the second, with the whole Qian trigram above it.

- Identify Tong Ren's upper and lower trigrams.

- Explain the structural relationship between Tong Ren and Da You.

- Explain why changing Qian's second line produces Tong Ren.

Tong Ren's lower trigram is Li, with solid top and bottom lines and a broken middle line. Above is Qian, with three solid lines. Only the second line is broken, at the center of the lower trigram. Changing Qian's second line to yin produces this hexagram.

Li conveys clarity, and Qian conveys shared unity. The single yielding line among five solid ones stands at the lower trigram's center, a centered position rather than a cramped gap. This structure is read as “fellowship”: different forces gather through a stable position in their midst, rather than by erasing differences.

Teaching example, not a prediction: Several groups with different positions agree to work together because an intermediary coordinator is accepted by all. Tong Ren describes this structure without guaranteeing smooth cooperation.

## The Six Lines: Original Text and Reflection

### Nine at the beginning: Fellowship at the gate. No blame.

Commentary on the Image: “Going out of the gate to join others—who could blame this?” Reaching others beyond one's own gate symbolizes cooperation not confined to one's household. Openness without favoritism is the reason for no blame. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Reaching others beyond one's own gate symbolizes cooperation not confined to one's household. Openness without favoritism is the reason for no blame.

For a new collaboration, first explain its purpose and boundaries openly and give people from different backgrounds room to participate. There is no need to rush into dividing insiders from outsiders.

Are you willing to let people outside your circle understand and participate in this undertaking?

### Six in the second place: Fellowship within the clan. Regret.

Commentary on the Image: “Fellowship within the clan is the way of regret.” Joining only relatives or close associates restricts cooperation through private ties; the warning of regret points to too narrow a view. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Joining only relatives or close associates restricts cooperation through private ties; the warning of regret points to too narrow a view.

Familiar company may feel easier, but choose partners by ability, principles, and actual responsibilities. Notice whether other suitable people are being overlooked.

Do you choose companions for a shared goal, or simply because you know each other?

### Nine in the third place: Concealing troops in the thickets, ascending the high hill. For three years, no rising to action.

Commentary on the Image: “Troops hide in the thickets because the enemy is strong. No rising for three years—how could one proceed?” Hiding soldiers and climbing to spy from above, yet not daring to act for a long time, depicts cooperation stalled by suspicion and opposition. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Hiding soldiers and climbing to spy from above, yet not daring to act for a long time, depicts cooperation stalled by suspicion and opposition.

If communication has become nothing but suspicion and defensiveness, establish the facts and clarify disagreements before deciding whether to cooperate. Do not try to advance through covert rivalry.

Which parts of your current defensiveness come from confirmed facts, and which are only assumptions?

### Nine in the fourth place: Mounting the wall, unable to prevail; attacking, good fortune.

Commentary on the Image: “Mounting the wall, one cannot prevail because rightness forbids it. Good fortune comes from returning to proper rules in difficulty.” Having climbed the wall, one stops the attack. The good fortune lies in reflecting on the situation and halting inappropriate confrontation, rather than succeeding by force. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Having climbed the wall, one stops the attack. The good fortune lies in reflecting on the situation and halting inappropriate confrontation, rather than succeeding by force.

As an argument escalates, withdrawing an excessive demand or stopping an attack does not mean abandoning your position. Leave room to negotiate again.

Can you stop an unnecessary confrontation before the conflict grows?

### Nine in the fifth place: In fellowship, first wailing, then laughing. Great armies overcome and meet.

Commentary on the Image: “Fellowship begins so because of central uprightness. Great armies meet: this speaks of overcoming one another.” Separation first brings cries, then reunion brings laughter: shared purpose connects only after obstacles. The original military imagery should not be taken directly as instructions for modern action. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Separation first brings cries, then reunion brings laughter: shared purpose connects only after obstacles. The original military imagery should not be taken directly as instructions for modern action.

Shared goals can still involve misunderstanding and resistance. Clarify differences and coordinate resources rather than treating temporary conflict as total rejection of you.

What goal do you truly share with the other person, and what obstacles remain unspoken?

### Nine at the top: Fellowship in the outskirts. No regret.

Commentary on the Image: “Fellowship in the outskirts: the purpose is not yet attained.” Meeting others outside the center reduces entanglements and regret, but the Image notes that the aim remains incompletely fulfilled and broad fellowship is still distant. (Zhouyi, Commentary on the Image; public domain)

Meeting others outside the center reduces entanglements and regret, but the Image notes that the aim remains incompletely fulfilled and broad fellowship is still distant.

Appropriate distance reduces friction, but deeper cooperation still requires recognizing your own withdrawal and deciding whether to participate more concretely.

Does the distance you maintain respect boundaries, or avoid a goal you once valued?

## Quick Quiz

- The second line

- The fifth line

- The third line

**The second line (Correct)** : Correct. The broken line in lower Li is its middle line, the second line of the whole hexagram.

**The fifth line** : A broken fifth line with all others solid forms Da You.

**The third line** : A broken third line forms Lu.

- Tong Ren · Fellowship

- Gou · Coming to Meet

- Da You · Great Possession

**Tong Ren · Fellowship (Correct)** : Correct. The lower trigram changes from Qian to Li; the upper remains Qian.

**Gou · Coming to Meet** : Gou results from changing the first line, not the second.

**Da You · Great Possession** : Da You's broken fifth line belongs to the upper trigram.

- Da You · Great Possession

- Qian · The Creative

- Lu · Treading

**Da You · Great Possession (Correct)** : Correct. Qian below and Li above form Hexagram 14, Da You. The broken line moves from the second to the fifth position.

**Qian · The Creative** : Exchanging positions does not make the broken line disappear.

**Lu · Treading** : Lu has Dui below and Qian above, with a broken third line.
