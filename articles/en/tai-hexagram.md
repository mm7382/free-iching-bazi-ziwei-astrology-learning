# What does the Tai hexagram mean? Hexagram 11 of the I Ching in plain words

> This is a mirror. The **canonical, most up-to-date version** is at https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/tai-hexagram.html

Written by [Wei Chun Chuang](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/about.html)・2026-11-13

*Tai is the eleventh hexagram of the I Ching: Earth above, Heaven below. Because the two meet, the hexagram means peace and free exchange. Plain-language notes on the judgment, the six lines, and where the saying "when Pi reaches its limit, Tai arrives" comes from.*

---

Tai is the eleventh of the sixty-four hexagrams: Kun ☷ (earth) above and Qian ☰ (heaven) below, so its full name is "Earth over Heaven". It sits directly before Pi, hexagram 12, which is its exact opposite, and the two together give the Chinese saying usually rendered "when blockage reaches its limit, peace arrives". This article explains the text line by line in plain words.

## What does Tai look like?

Three yang lines below (Qian, heaven) and three yin lines above (Kun, earth). Earth sitting on top of heaven looks odd until you notice the point: heaven's energy rises and earth's energy sinks, so with heaven underneath the two move towards each other and meet. Meeting is exchange, and exchange is what Tai names.

Pi, the next hexagram, reverses this: heaven above and earth below, each moving away from the other, so nothing meets. If you want the trigram symbols first, see [the eight trigrams](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/eight-trigrams.html).

## What does the judgment say?

The judgment is short: "Tai: the small goes, the great comes; good fortune, success."

- The small goes: "small" means yin, and the yin lines have moved out to the upper trigram.

- The great comes: "great" means yang, and the yang lines have come into the inner trigram, the side nearer to you.

- Good fortune, success: favourable and free-flowing.

What has to leave has left and what should arrive has arrived, so things move. Traditionally this is the image of heaven and earth in contact, of people above and below actually talking to each other, of work that can get done.

## What do the six lines say?

The lines trace an open period from its start, through its height, to the turn that always follows. "Nine" marks a yang line and "six" a yin line; the positions run from first at the bottom to top at the head.

- First nine: "Pulling up grass brings up the roots of its kind with it; to set out brings good fortune." In a good period you do not move alone — going together is favourable.

- Nine in the second place: "Bearing with the uncultivated, crossing the river on foot, not leaving the distant behind, letting your faction go — thus one accords with the middle way." Breadth, nerve, reach and no favouritism.

- Nine in the third place: "No level ground without a slope, no going without a return. Staying upright through hardship brings no blame. Do not grieve over your sincerity; there is good fortune in your food." Nothing level stays level.

- Six in the fourth place: "Fluttering down, not wealthy, together with the neighbour, trusting without warning each other." Movement rests on trust between neighbours rather than on riches.

- Six in the fifth place: "Di Yi gives his daughter in marriage; this brings blessing and supreme good fortune." Someone in the higher position willingly comes down to join with those below.

- Six at the top: "The wall falls back into the moat. Do not use the army now. Make your announcement from your own town. Perseverance brings regret." The open period has run out, and the next hexagram is Pi.

## Where does "heaven and earth meet" come from?

From the Image commentary on Tai: "Heaven and earth meet: Tai. Thus the ruler shapes and completes the way of heaven and earth, assists what is fitting in heaven and earth, and so helps the people."

## Where the saying actually comes from

The phrase is often quoted as if it were a line of the original text. It is not. Tai is hexagram 11 and Pi is hexagram 12; their trigrams are exactly swapped, one open and one blocked, and they sit side by side in the sequence. The sequence itself carries the idea that a peak turns and a trough turns back, and later readers compressed that into four characters. The top line of Tai, the wall falling back into the moat, is the moment of the turn.

## What is Tai useful for?

Read it less as "things are going well" and more as instructions for an open period: move with others, stay broad without forming a clique, expect the level ground to tilt, rely on trust rather than money, be willing to come down from your position, and stop pushing once the wall starts falling. For looking a hexagram up by name, see [how to look up a hexagram](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/hexagram-lookup.html); for the pair most often read together, see [Qian](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/qian-hexagram.html) and [Kun](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/kun-hexagram.html).

## FAQ

**Is Tai a good hexagram?**

Its judgment, "the small goes, the great comes; good fortune, success", is favourable. But the third line warns that no level ground stays level, and the top line shows the city wall falling back into the moat. Tai is about how to keep an open period open, not a promise that things stay easy.

**Where does "when Pi reaches its limit, Tai arrives" come from?**

Pi is hexagram 12, directly after Tai, and means blockage where Tai means free exchange. The saying comes from the way the two hexagrams sit next to each other as opposites in the sequence, not from a single line of the original text.

**Why is Earth above and Heaven below in Tai?**

Heaven's energy rises and earth's energy sinks. With heaven above and earth below they move apart and never meet. Tai puts Kun (earth) on top and Qian (heaven) underneath, so the two move towards each other and meet in the middle — hence "peace" in the sense of free exchange.
