Qian is the first of the sixty-four hexagrams and the one people ask about most. All six lines are yang and both trigrams are Qian, so its full name is "Qian is Heaven". This article explains its text line by line in plain words.

## What does Qian look like?

Six solid yang lines: Qian ☰ above and Qian ☰ below. The trigram's image is heaven and its quality is strength that keeps moving without stopping.

Its counterpart is Kun, six yin lines, the image of earth and receptiveness. The two are often treated as the starting point of the whole book.

## What does the judgment "yuan heng li zhen" mean?

Qian's judgment is four characters. A common plain reading:

- Yuan: origin, what is fundamental.

- Heng: getting through, flowing freely.

- Li: fitting, beneficial.

- Zhen: correct and steadfast.

The Words of the Text commentary reads them as four virtues. Some scholars think the earliest sense was closer to "great success; it is favourable to stay steadfast". Both readings are common; as a beginner, keep the gist: a good beginning that goes through, as long as you stay on course.

## What do the six lines say?

The lines follow a dragon from hiding to flying to going too far, describing how something develops from its start to its peak. "Nine" in a line name means a yang line; first, second and so on to top give its position from the bottom.

- First nine: "Hidden dragon. Do not act." The time is not ripe; build strength first.

- Second nine: "Dragon appearing in the field. It is favourable to see the great person." You start to be seen; seek someone who can guide you.

- Third nine: "The noble one is active all day and still wary at night. Danger, but no blame." Steady effort and alertness keep you from error.

- Fourth nine: "Perhaps leaping in the depths. No blame." You may try to rise or step back; reading the moment correctly is not a mistake.

- Fifth nine: "Flying dragon in the heavens. It is favourable to see the great person." The best position, where your strengths can work.

- Top nine: "Arrogant dragon will have regret." Going too high brings regret; at a peak, know when to hold back.

## What is "using nines"?

Qian has one extra statement: "A flight of dragons without a head. Good fortune." Strength that does not push to be first works out well. Only Qian has this; Kun has a matching "using sixes".

## Where does "heaven moves with strength" come from?

From Qian's Image commentary: heaven moves vigorously without rest, and the noble one likewise strengthens themselves without ceasing. It has become a widely quoted motto.

## What can you learn from Qian?

The six lines make a good exercise in timing. Is your situation more like hiding, being newly seen, working hard, getting ready to leap, being in the right place, or having gone too far? Read this way, the hexagram is more useful than a verdict of good or bad luck.

## FAQ

**Is Qian a good hexagram?**

Its judgment is broadly positive, but the top line warns that going too far brings regret. Every hexagram mixes favourable conditions with cautions, so good-or-bad is not a useful way to read it.

**What does "hidden dragon, do not act" mean?**

The dragon is still under water and the time is not ripe. Hold off, build your strength and wait for the right moment.

**Why is the fifth line special?**

The fifth line is traditionally the most honoured position in a hexagram. Qian's fifth line, the flying dragon, later gave Chinese a common phrase for the throne.
