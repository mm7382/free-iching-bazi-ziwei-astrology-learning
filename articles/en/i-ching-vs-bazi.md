# I Ching or Bazi: what is the difference and which to learn first

> This is a mirror. The **canonical, most up-to-date version** is at https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/i-ching-vs-bazi.html

Written by [Wei Chun Chuang](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/about.html)・2026-11-24

*The I Ching and Bazi are often grouped together, but their inputs, methods and questions differ: Bazi builds a fixed four-pillar chart from your birth time, while the I Ching casts a hexagram for one question now. A neutral comparison of sources, tools, what each can answer, difficulty, and how to choose.*

---

The I Ching and Bazi often end up in the same mental drawer, filed under “fortune telling”. In practice they take **different inputs, work in different ways, and answer different questions**. Sorting out the difference first saves a lot of time later.

## The difference in one line

**Bazi reads a fixed underlying chart; the I Ching asks about one thing happening now.**

Bazi takes your year, month, day and hour of birth and builds four pairs of stems and branches — eight characters. That chart never changes, and it speaks to disposition and the long structure of a life. The I Ching needs no birth data at all: you bring a specific question and cast a hexagram on the spot, and it speaks to where that one thing stands and where it might go.

## Point by point

- **Input:** Bazi needs the year, month, day and hour of birth; without an accurate hour the hour pillar is undetermined. The I Ching needs no personal data, only a clearly stated question.

- **Tool:** Bazi is calendar lookup and solar-term conversion — a **deterministic calculation**, where the same birth time always gives the same chart. The I Ching is three coins thrown six times — **random sampling**, where asking the same question again gives a different hexagram.

- **Unit:** Bazi works from ten heavenly stems and twelve earthly branches, then the Five Phases and the Ten Gods. The I Ching works from yin and yang lines, three to a trigram and two trigrams to a hexagram.

- **What it answers:** Bazi describes long-run tendencies, such as whether the Day Master is strong and which phase dominates. The I Ching describes one situation and what to watch in it: whether to advance or hold, whether to talk first or prepare first.

- **How often you use it:** A Bazi chart is built once, then read against luck pillars and annual cycles. The I Ching is cast each time something comes up, so it gets used far more often.

## They are hard in different places

**Bazi is steep early and steady later.** You have to learn the ten stems, the twelve branches, the Five Phase cycles and solar-term month changes before you can build a chart at all; once you can, the rest deepens gradually.

**The I Ching is flat early and deep later.** The casting rules take ten minutes, and the structure of lines, trigrams and hexagrams is clear. The hard part is reading the judgment and line texts, a body of commentary three thousand years deep that you can keep reading for as long as you like.

Bazi has a barrier at the door; the I Ching has a barrier at the bottom.

## Which to start with

- **You want to understand long-run tendencies** and you know your exact birth hour: start with Bazi.

- **You want a method for thinking through a specific problem**, or you do not know your birth hour: start with the I Ching.

- **You are interested in classical texts**: start with the I Ching. It is a classic in its own right, with judgments, line texts and the Ten Wings to read.

- **You prefer a system with explicit rules you can derive step by step**: start with Bazi. Building the chart is pure rule-following, with no random element.

## What both share

Whichever you pick, some things hold for both. They are **languages for reflection and self-observation**, not testable predictive tools; no controlled study shows either predicting specific events. Medical, legal and financial decisions belong with professionals, not with a chart or a hexagram.

Both also have several schools, and the same chart or hexagram will be read differently by different teachers. That is not fraud; it follows from readings resting on tradition and judgement rather than measurement. Treat anyone who says only their school is correct with some reserve.

## Can you use both?

Yes, as long as the division of labour is clear: Bazi for “what kind of person am I broadly, and what stage am I in”, the I Ching for “how should I see this one thing now”. Do not use one to verify the other. Their premises differ, and cross-checking mostly confirms what you already wanted to believe.

## Common questions

**What is the main difference between the I Ching and Bazi?**

Bazi builds a fixed four-pillar chart from your year, month, day and hour of birth and speaks to long-run disposition and life structure. The I Ching needs no birth data and casts a hexagram for one specific question now, speaking to where that situation stands. One is a fixed underlying chart; the other is a question in the moment.

**Which can I learn if I do not know my birth hour?**

The I Ching. It needs no personal data at all, only a clearly stated question. Without an accurate hour, the hour pillar of a Bazi chart is undetermined and the reading is affected.

**Which is easier to start with?**

Bazi is steeper at the start: you need the ten stems, twelve branches, Five Phase cycles and solar-term month changes before you can build a chart, after which it deepens steadily. The I Ching's casting rules take ten minutes; its difficulty lies in reading the judgment and line texts. Choose the kind of difficulty you mind less.

**Can I use both together?**

Yes, with a clear division of labour: Bazi for what kind of person you broadly are and what stage you are in, the I Ching for how to see one particular thing now. Do not use one to verify the other; their premises differ and cross-checking tends only to confirm what you already believed.
