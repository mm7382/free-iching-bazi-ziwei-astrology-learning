# What are the twelve houses in a birth chart? A beginner's guide

Besides the sign each planet is in, a birth chart shows which house it falls in. Houses tell you which area of life a planet's energy mainly shows up in. This article explains what the twelve houses are, the basic theme of each, and a few questions beginners often ask.

## What are the houses in a birth chart?

A birth chart is a map of the sky at the moment you were born. Signs divide the sky along the ecliptic into twelve equal thirty-degree segments; houses divide it into twelve areas based on the time and place of birth, using the horizon and meridian as reference. Put simply, signs describe how, and houses describe in which area of life.

## What does each of the twelve houses represent?

- 1st house: self, outward image, first impressions. Its cusp is the Ascendant.

- 2nd house: money, personal resources and values.

- 3rd house: communication, learning, siblings and everyday contacts.

- 4th house: home, roots and private life. Its cusp is the IC.

- 5th house: creativity, play, romance and children.

- 6th house: daily work, routines and looking after your health.

- 7th house: partners, collaborators and one-to-one relationships. Its cusp is the Descendant.

- 8th house: shared resources, intimacy and deep transformation.

- 9th house: higher education, philosophy and long journeys.

- 10th house: career, social role and public image. Its cusp is the Midheaven.

- 11th house: friends, groups and hopes for the future.

- 12th house: solitude, the unconscious and what is less visible.

## What are angular, succedent and cadent houses?

The houses are traditionally grouped in three. The 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th are angular: their cusps are the four angles, Ascendant, IC, Descendant and Midheaven, and they are usually seen as the most prominent positions. The 2nd, 5th, 8th and 11th are succedent; the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th are cadent. This grouping helps you spot a chart's emphasis quickly.

## Why do houses need an accurate birth time?

The Earth turns once a day, so the Ascendant moves into a new sign roughly every two hours and the houses move with it. A difference of an hour or two can put a planet in a completely different house. Without a birth time, the signs of the planets are mostly still usable, but the Ascendant and houses cannot be fixed.

## What are house systems, and why do they give different results?

There are several ways to calculate the twelve divisions, called house systems. Common ones include Placidus, Whole Sign, Equal and Koch. Placidus is widely used today; Whole Sign treats the entire sign containing the Ascendant as the 1st house and is common in classical astrology. Under different systems the same planet can land in neighbouring houses, so when comparing charts, first make sure they use the same system.

## What is an empty house?

With ten planets spread over twelve houses, some houses will always be empty. An empty house does not mean that area of life is unimportant, only that no planet emphasises it. To read one, look at the sign on its cusp and where that sign's ruling planet is placed.

## How should a beginner start reading houses?

Find your Ascendant and 1st house, then see which houses your Sun and Moon fall in and think about the themes above. Looking at one or two planets at a time is easier than trying to read all twelve houses at once.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between signs and houses?**

Signs are twelve equal segments of the ecliptic and describe the style of an energy; houses are twelve areas based on birth time and place and describe which area of life it shows up in.

**Can I see my houses without a birth time?**

Not reliably. The Ascendant changes sign roughly every two hours and the houses move with it, so without an accurate time you cannot tell which house a planet is in.

**Why do different websites give different houses?**

Usually because they use different house systems, such as Placidus and Whole Sign, or because the birth time or place entered differs.
