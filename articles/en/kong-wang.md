# What does kong wang (void emptiness) mean in a Bazi chart?

> This is a mirror. The **canonical, most up-to-date version** is at https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/kong-wang.html

Written by [Wei Chun Chuang](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/about.html)・2026-11-23

*Kong wang, or xun kong, are the two branches a ten-day cycle cannot pair with a stem. The table for all six cycles, how to find yours, and what it is not.*

---

Anyone reading Bazi or casting with the six lines runs into the phrase sooner or later: this pillar is void. This page explains where kong wang comes from, gives the table for all six ten-day cycles, shows how to find your own, how the tradition reads it, and why it is not the empty palace of Zi Wei Dou Shu.

What follows is this site's synthesis of the inherited interpretive tradition, not a single standard answer. The site introduces this language as a tool for reflection, with no claim that it predicts events. For medical, legal or financial decisions, consult a qualified professional.

## Where it comes from

The answer is in the [structure of stems and branches](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/bazi-four-pillars.html). There are ten heavenly stems and twelve earthly branches. Pair them one by one and the stems run out after ten, leaving two branches with no stem to go with. Those two leftover branches are the void branches of that ten-day cycle, called kong wang, or xun kong.

So it is not a verdict anyone assigned. It is the gap that pairing ten against twelve must leave. The sexagenary cycle divides neatly into six such cycles, and each has its own pair.

## The table for the six cycles

| Cycle | Range | Void branches |
|---|---|---|
| Jia Zi | Jia Zi to Gui You | Xu, Hai |
| Jia Xu | Jia Xu to Gui Wei | Shen, You |
| Jia Shen | Jia Shen to Gui Si | Wu, Wei |
| Jia Wu | Jia Wu to Gui Mao | Chen, Si |
| Jia Chen | Jia Chen to Gui Chou | Yin, Mao |
| Jia Yin | Jia Yin to Gui Hai | Zi, Chou |

You can check it yourself. The Jia Zi cycle runs from Jia Zi to Gui You and uses the branches Zi, Chou, Yin, Mao, Chen, Si, Wu, Wei, Shen and You. The two never used are Xu and Hai. Every cycle can be verified the same way.

## Finding your own

- Write down your day pillar. If that is new, start with [what the Day Master is](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/day-master.html).

- Count back to the pillar of that run that begins with the stem Jia. That tells you the cycle.

- Read the two void branches from the table, then check whether your year, month or [hour branch](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/earthly-branches-hours.html) sits on either of them.

For example, a day pillar of Bing Yin counts back Bing Yin, Yi Chou, Jia Zi, so it belongs to the Jia Zi cycle and its void branches are Xu and Hai. In that chart, any year, month or hour pillar carrying Xu or Hai is read as void. The day pillar itself is usually excluded, since the reckoning starts from it.

Two further notes. Some schools derive a second set from the year pillar and read both alongside each other. Others separate a full void from a half void depending on whether the day stem and the void branch share the same polarity, treating the matching case as the heavier one. When two sources disagree, ask which reckoning each used.

## How the tradition reads it

The common misreading is to treat a void branch as bad luck. The tradition treats it as a mark of something not being solid, so the usual phrasing is that favourable readings are discounted and unfavourable ones softened. What carries meaning is which pillar it lands on, because each pillar stands for a different area of life: the year pillar for the older generation and the early environment, the month pillar for upbringing and siblings, the hour pillar for later years and children. A void branch says that area is harder to get a firm hold on.

It can also be undone. Most schools hold that a clash or a combination involving the void branch fills it in and restores part of its force, and that a [luck pillar](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/luck-pillars.html) or annual pillar arriving on that branch is read the same way. How much is restored is not agreed on, and that disagreement is normal.

## Void branches in six-line divination

Kong wang is used more directly in six-line divination than in Bazi. The caster first fixes the void branches of the day, and if the line standing for the matter asked about falls on one of them, the tradition reads it as not yet formed, the timing not ripe, or the other party not giving it thought, rather than as failure. Waiting until the void has passed, or until a clash or combination fills it in, before reading again is the usual handling. Coming from the I Ching side, start with [how to interpret a reading](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/interpret-a-reading.html).

## Not the same as an empty palace

These two are easy to confuse, and they come from different places:

- **Kong wang** comes from the stem and branch pairing of the sexagenary cycle. It is a pair of leftover branches, and it belongs to Bazi and to divination.

- **An empty palace** comes from the star layout of the [twelve palaces](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/ziwei-twelve-palaces.html) in Zi Wei Dou Shu. It means a palace with none of the [fourteen major stars](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/ziwei-fourteen-major-stars.html) in it, read by borrowing from the opposite palace.

One looks at stems and branches, the other at stars. Both happen to use the word empty in Chinese. Some Zi Wei schools do also list the void branches among their minor stars, which is one more reason to be clear about which one you mean.

## Three common misunderstandings

- **A void branch means nothing is there.** The character is still in the chart and its interactions still apply. The tradition only says its force is thin.

- **People with void branches have bad charts.** Every chart has its own pair of void branches, so one or two showing up among the other pillars is ordinary, not an exception.

- **The table has to be memorised.** It does not. Once you know it is ten stems against twelve branches leaving two over, you can derive it whenever you need it.

## Common questions

**What does kong wang mean?**

They are the two earthly branches that cannot be paired with a heavenly stem inside a ten-day cycle. Ten stems meet twelve branches, so two are always left over. It describes a gap in the pairing, not a good or bad omen.

**How do I find my own void branches?**

Find which of the six cycles your day pillar belongs to and read off that cycle's two leftover branches, then check whether the year, month or hour branch falls on one of them.

**Is a void branch in my chart bad news?**

The tradition does not read it as bad. It says the position is less solid, so favourable readings are discounted and unfavourable ones softened. Which pillar it lands on is what carries meaning.

**Is it the same as an empty palace in Zi Wei Dou Shu?**

No. One is computed from stem and branch pairing, the other from where the fourteen major stars fall. They share the word empty and nothing else.

**Can a void branch be cancelled?**

Most schools say it can be filled in by a clash or a combination, or by a luck pillar or annual pillar arriving on that branch. Schools disagree on how much is restored.

**What does it mean in six-line divination?**

That the matter is not yet formed or the timing is not ripe, rather than that it fails. Reading again after the void has passed is the usual handling.
