When reading a Bazi chart, the first character to find is the Day Master. It is the chart's reference point: the Five Phases relationships and the Ten Gods are all worked out from it. This article explains what it is, how to find it and what to look at next.

## What does Day Master mean?

A Bazi chart has four pillars, year, month, day and hour, each with one heavenly stem and one earthly branch, for eight characters in total. The heavenly stem of the day pillar is called the Day Master, also the day stem. Traditionally it represents "me" in the chart, and the other seven characters are described relative to it.

## How do you find your Day Master?

The steps are simple:

- Get your full four pillars. They require a calendar conversion and are usually calculated with a perpetual calendar or a chart tool.

- Find the third pillar, the day pillar.

- The top character of the day pillar, its heavenly stem, is your Day Master.

If someone's day pillar is Bing-Wu, the Day Master is Bing; if it is Gui-Mao, the Day Master is Gui. It is not the year stem, and it is not the zodiac animal.

## Which phase does each of the ten stems belong to?

The Day Master is always one of the ten stems, each with a phase and a polarity:

- Jia 甲 (yang Wood), Yi 乙 (yin Wood)

- Bing 丙 (yang Fire), Ding 丁 (yin Fire)

- Wu 戊 (yang Earth), Ji 己 (yin Earth)

- Geng 庚 (yang Metal), Xin 辛 (yin Metal)

- Ren 壬 (yang Water), Gui 癸 (yin Water)

So a Bing Day Master can also be called a yang Fire Day Master. Common images compare Jia to a tall tree, Yi to grass and flowers, Bing to the sun and Ding to a candle. These are memory aids, and books differ in the details.

## How is the Day Pillar determined?

Day pillars follow the sixty-day cycle from Jia-Zi and Yi-Chou through to Gui-Hai, moving one step each day regardless of the lengths of lunar months. The case to watch is a birth late at night near the Zi hour, which runs from 11 p.m. to 1 a.m. Schools differ on whether the day changes at 11 p.m., so the day pillar and Day Master can differ. If you were born in that window, find out which rule your tool uses.

## Why does Bazi reading start from the Day Master?

Because every relationship in the chart is described relative to it. Based on the Five Phases cycles, each other stem relates to the Day Master in one of five ways: same as me, produced by me, controlled by me, controlling me, or producing me. Split by matching or opposite polarity, these become the Ten Gods. Without the Day Master you cannot tell what role any other character plays for you.

## What should you look at after finding it?

Beginners can do two things. First, remember your Day Master's phase and polarity. Second, check which phase each of the other seven characters belongs to and whether it produces, controls or matches your Day Master. That is the first step to reading a Bazi chart.

## FAQ

**Is the Day Master the same as the Chinese zodiac animal?**

No. The zodiac animal comes from the year pillar's branch; the Day Master is the day pillar's stem. They sit in different places in the chart.

**Does everyone born on the same day share a Day Master?**

With the same day-change rule, people born on the same day share a day pillar and Day Master. Differences arise near the Zi hour, where schools use different rules.

**Is a Day Master strong or weak?**

Traditionally readers assess how much support the Day Master receives from the rest of the chart. That is an advanced topic; beginners should first find it and learn its phase and polarity.
