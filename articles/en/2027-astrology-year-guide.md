# The 2027 Astrology Year Guide: the astrological year starts at the equinox, not 1 January

*In Western astrology the year begins when the Sun enters Aries at the spring equinox, around 20 March 2027 — not 1 January, not Lichun on 4 February, and not Lunar New Year on 6 February. This guide compares all four year boundaries, gives the full element, modality and ruling planet table for the twelve signs, and explains how fast each planet changes sign.*

---

**The short answer: in Western astrology, 2027 does not begin on 1 January. It begins the moment the Sun enters Aries — the spring equinox — which in 2027 falls around 20 March.** That is not the same day as Lichun in Bazi (around 4 February) or Lunar New Year (6 February) either.

This page does not predict what will happen to each sign in 2027. It teaches something that lasts longer: where the structure of an astrological year comes from, how four systems disagree about when a year starts, and how to look things up yourself instead of waiting to be handed a forecast.

*This article explains the framework and concepts of Western astrology. It is not a personal prediction. For medical, legal or financial decisions, consult a qualified professional.*

## The year starts at the equinox because of how the zodiac is defined

Western astrology uses the **tropical zodiac**. Its origin point is not a star but the **equinox point** — the place on the celestial sphere where the Sun crosses the celestial equator moving north. That point is defined as **0 degrees Aries**.

So "the Sun enters Aries" and "the spring equinox" name the same event: the Sun completing one circuit of the ecliptic and returning to the start. The annual cycle begins there and runs through Taurus, Gemini and on to Pisces.

In 2027 the equinox falls **around 20 March**. Two things are worth noting: the equinox is a **moment**, not a whole day, and because time zones differ, that same moment can fall on 19, 20 or 21 March depending on where you are. For hour-level precision you need astronomical data for that year.

This also settles a common puzzle: **why Sun sign dates shift by a day between years.** A tropical year is about 365.2422 days, not a whole number, so the moment the Sun reaches 0 degrees Aries drifts about six hours later each year and leap years pull it back. If you were born on a cusp, use the actual moment for your birth year, not a fixed table.

## Four systems, four year boundaries: when 2027 starts

This table is only visible when you put all four of this site's subjects side by side. Four articles about "2027" are not describing the same stretch of time, and their reasons differ completely.

| System | Start of 2027 | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Civil calendar | 1 January | An administrative convention, with no astronomical basis |
| Bazi | Lichun, around 4 February | The twenty-four solar terms; the Sun reaches 315 degrees of ecliptic longitude |
| Lunar calendar and zodiac animal | First lunar month, day 1: 6 February | The lunar month; day 1 is the day of the new moon |
| Western astrology | Equinox, around 20 March | The Sun returns to 0 degrees of the ecliptic, the start of Aries |

Three practical consequences:

- If you read year-ahead articles from more than one system, **check which stretch of time each one means**, or you will overlay two different years.

- People born between January and March are the ones most often mis-assigned. A Bazi year pillar and a zodiac animal can legitimately disagree — see [Can you build a Bazi chart from a lunar birthday?](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/lunar-calendar-bazi.html)

- All four dates are **astronomical or calendrical facts**, not the claim of one school, so you can verify them yourself.

## The three fixed properties of every sign: element, modality, ruling planet

Year-ahead articles drop lines like "fire signs this year" without saying what that means. Each sign has three properties that never change with the year: the element describes its basic quality, the modality describes how it acts, and the ruling planet is the planet traditionally assigned to it.

| Sign | Element | Modality | Modern ruler | Traditional ruler |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aries | Fire | Cardinal | Mars | Mars |
| Taurus | Earth | Fixed | Venus | Venus |
| Gemini | Air | Mutable | Mercury | Mercury |
| Cancer | Water | Cardinal | Moon | Moon |
| Leo | Fire | Fixed | Sun | Sun |
| Virgo | Earth | Mutable | Mercury | Mercury |
| Libra | Air | Cardinal | Venus | Venus |
| Scorpio | Water | Fixed | Pluto | Mars |
| Sagittarius | Fire | Mutable | Jupiter | Jupiter |
| Capricorn | Earth | Cardinal | Saturn | Saturn |
| Aquarius | Air | Fixed | Uranus | Saturn |
| Pisces | Water | Mutable | Neptune | Jupiter |

Each element covers three signs and each modality covers four, and the twelve combinations come out without a repeat. Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces have different modern and traditional rulers because Uranus, Neptune and Pluto were only found in the telescope era; ancient astrology used the seven bodies visible to the naked eye. When two books disagree here, that is usually why.

For more on these two axes, see [What are the four elements and three modalities of the zodiac signs?](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/elements-modalities.html)

## How fast each planet changes sign decides what a year-ahead can honestly cover

Year-ahead articles are possible at all because the slow planets barely move within one year. Roughly how long each body takes to cross one sign:

- **Moon**: about two and a half days. No annual article can account for it; look up the day.

- **Sun**: about a month, covering all twelve signs in a year.

- **Mercury and Venus**: weeks to months, with the pace changing around retrograde periods.

- **Mars**: about six to seven weeks.

- **Jupiter**: about a year per sign, so it usually changes once within a year.

- **Saturn**: about two and a half years per sign.

- **Uranus**: about seven years per sign.

- **Neptune**: about fourteen years per sign.

- **Pluto**: anywhere from a dozen to over twenty years, because its orbit is markedly eccentric and it lingers far longer in some signs than others.

So a credible year-ahead talks about the slower configurations and does not claim to know where your Moon or Ascendant is on a given day. **To find a specific body on a specific date, use an ephemeris** — the day-by-day table of zodiacal positions that every astrology app computes from. This article deliberately does not list 2027 positions, because those have to come from astronomical data for that year, and a copied list is worse than none.

One more thing that gets garbled every year: **a retrograde planet is not actually moving backwards.** It is an appearance created by the relative motion of Earth and that planet. See [What does retrograde actually mean?](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/retrograde.html)

## Four steps to read 2027 into your own chart

- **Get your birth chart calculated.** You need the date, time and place. Without a time there are workarounds — see [Can you read a birth chart without knowing your birth time?](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/unknown-birth-time.html)

- **Find your Sun, Moon and Ascendant.** These are the starting points for reading a chart — see [How to read a birth chart: the Sun, Moon and Ascendant](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/sun-moon-rising.html)

- **Map the year's slow planets onto your own houses.** The same configuration lands in different houses for different people, which is exactly why one year-ahead cannot fit everyone. See [What are the twelve houses in a birth chart?](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/twelve-houses.html)

- **Look at the angles.** Aspects between planets, and between planets and your natal positions, carry most of the interpretation — see [What are aspects in a birth chart?](https://mm7382.github.io/learn/en/aspects.html)

## Three things not to misunderstand

- **A twelve-sign year-ahead carries very little information.** It uses one position, the Sun, and sorts everyone into twelve groups. Two people with the same Sun sign can have completely different Moons, Ascendants and houses.

- **Positions do not determine events.** A chart describes positions and angles. Any claim that a given year makes something inevitable goes beyond what astrology can say.

- **Different year boundaries do not mean somebody got it wrong.** The civil calendar, the solar terms, the lunar month and the tropical zodiac each have their own basis. What matters is knowing which one you are reading.
